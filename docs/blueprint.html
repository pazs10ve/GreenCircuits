System design · v0.4 · 27 Sep 2026

GreenCircuits

A research and strategy-testing platform for NSE and BSE equities, F&O, MCX commodities, bonds and IPOs, designed for 100,000 requests an hour and 1,000 concurrent users. It is built as a portfolio project. The whole stack runs on one machine for close to nothing, load tests prove the scale, and a production profile sits alongside for when someone asks how it would grow.

§1Where it runs

Answer Build on your laptop, show it on free tiers, and rent cloud machines only to prove the numbers.
Build · your laptop $0

Docker Compose runs the whole stack: TimescaleDB, Valkey, the API, stream gateway, ingestor, alert engine, workers and Grafana. A built-in market simulator feeds it at night and at weekends.

Show · a public link $0–5 a month

The web app on Vercel's free Hobby plan. The backend on one small VM behind Caddy and Cloudflare's free plan: Oracle's Always Free ARM instance (2 cores, 12 GB since June 2026) or a VPS of about $5. Or leave it off and start it before an interview.

Prove · a load test ≈$1–2 a run

Start a 4-vCPU VM for two hours, run k6 with 1,000 WebSocket clients and the 100k-an-hour request mix, publish the results, then destroy it. A new AWS account's $100–200 of credits covers many runs.

The web app can live on a serverless platform; the backend cannot. The reason makes a good interview answer. Vercel added WebSocket support in June 2026 (public beta, Node.js on Fluid compute), and it still does not fit:

  • A socket lives inside one function instance and closes when the function reaches its maximum duration: 300 seconds on the free Hobby plan, 800 on Pro, 1,800 in a beta. A user watching F&O from 09:10 to 15:45 would be disconnected and resubscribed 29 times.
  • Fan-out across instances still needs an external pub/sub such as Redis, and memory and transfer are billed for the full life of every open socket.
  • The feed ingestor must be exactly one long-running process holding a single feed connection from 08:55 until MCX closes. Vercel has no always-on worker. Cron runs at most once a minute, and each run stops at the same duration limit.
  • The candle builder and alert engine keep state in memory all session. Serverless instances are recycled whenever the platform decides.

For production. If real users ever arrived, each container maps onto an AWS managed service (§10.3). That costs about $2,300–2,700 a month at launch, mostly for a highly available database, and exchange licences for real-time prices would cost more than the servers (§6). You will not run that profile. It is your answer when an interviewer asks how this would scale.

Deliberately left out for now

  • Managed cloud services. They bill every hour whether or not anyone uses the site. §10.3 lists what each container becomes in production.
  • Kubernetes. Compose on one host, or ECS Fargate in production, runs a handful of services with far less to operate.
  • Kafka. Valkey pub/sub and Postgres cover this volume. Move to NATS JetStream or Kafka when you need replayable streams or ten times the tick volume.
  • ClickHouse. Add it when you keep raw ticks for analytics. It is available in ap-south-1.
  • Microservices. Modules inside one API first; extract one when it needs its own scaling or release cadence.
  • GraphQL. Plain REST responses cache cleanly at the CDN, which is where most of the 100k requests an hour should end.

§3Scope and specs

3.1What each asset class gets

NSE · BSE

Equities and indices

Quotes and charts from 1 minute to monthly, adjusted for splits and bonuses. Ten or more years of financials and ratios, quarterly results, shareholding and promoter pledges, corporate actions, exchange filings, peers, delivery %, bulk and block deals, insider trades, ASM/GSM flags, sector heatmaps, breadth and FPI/DII flows.

NSE · BSE · equity, currency, rates

F&O

Option chain with OI, change in OI, IV and Greeks. PCR, max pain and ATM IV history, OI buildup (long buildup, short covering and the rest), futures basis and rollover, IV rank, participant-wise OI, futures-equivalent OI and the ban list, and a strategy builder with payoff at expiry and today. Currency and interest-rate futures use the same model.

MCX · NCDEX

Commodities

Futures and options on futures, term structure across expiries, the same option-chain component, spot references, and contract specs: lot, quotation unit, delivery terms and each contract's own trading hours (agri closes at 17:00).

G-secs · SDLs · T-bills · SGBs · corporate

Bonds

A bond screener by rating, YTM, maturity, coupon and security. Cash-flow schedules, rating history, the G-sec yield curve against a month and a year ago, and a price ↔ YTM calculator.

Mainboard · SME

IPOs

Calendar, price band, lot and minimum investment, live category-wise subscription, dates from open to T+3 listing, RHP financials and documents, and listing-day performance. Facts only: an apply or avoid opinion is regulated research (§6).

Every signed-in user

Watchlists, alerts, portfolio

Watchlists that stream. Alerts on price, % move, volume, OI, IV, indicators, new screen matches, results dates and IPO milestones. A portfolio tracker with CSV and CAS import, FIFO P&L and XIRR. Notifications in app, by web push and by email.

3.2Strategy lab: build, backtest, paper trade, plan

One strategy definition moves through four stages. You build it, backtest it on history, paper trade it on the live feed, and hold yourself to a written plan. The same engine runs the backtest and the paper trading, so their results can be compared.

StageWhat a user can doNotes
BuildWrite entry and exit rules in the screener's expression language, extended with crossovers and indicator functions. Exit on a target, stop-loss, trailing stop, time of day or opposite signal. Size positions by fixed rupees, % of equity or risk per trade. Choose a universe: one stock, a list, a saved screen, or an index's members as they were on each date. Option strategies pick legs by moneyness or delta and expiry, for example "sell the ATM straddle at 09:20 on expiry day, 25% stop per leg, exit at 15:15".Definitions are versioned. Editing adds a version, so old results keep pointing at the rules that produced them.
BacktestRun on daily or 1-minute bars over any range that has data. Results: equity and drawdown curves, CAGR, Sharpe, Sortino, maximum drawdown, win rate, profit factor, exposure, a trade list, monthly returns and a NIFTY 50 TRI benchmark, split into in-sample and out-of-sample periods.Point-in-time data: universes, lot sizes, expiries, price bands and ban days as they were, Indian charges from dated rate tables, and fills on the next bar with slippage.
OptimiseSweep parameters with walk-forward windows.Stretch goal. Results are ranked by out-of-sample score, never by the best in-sample one.
Paper tradeDeploy any strategy version to a virtual account on the live or simulated feed, or place manual orders: market, limit, SL and SL-M. Positions and P&L stream like a watchlist.No real orders. Routing real orders would bring in SEBI's rules for retail algorithmic trading.
PlanA trading plan sets capital, risk per trade, maximum daily loss, maximum open positions, allowed setups and a pre-trade checklist; it is checked against the paper account and paired with a trade journal. An investment plan sets a goal, a date, a monthly SIP and a yearly step-up, and is replayed over historical index returns to show a range of outcomes.The user supplies every assumption and instrument. The platform simulates and never recommends, which keeps it a tool rather than investment advice.

§4Capacity math

100,000 requests an hour is a modest load. The harder numbers are the tick stream coming in and the frames going out, so those are sized separately.

Storage≈3 GB/day raw, about a tenth after compression≈75 GB/year

All of this fits on one 4-vCPU machine with 8–16 GB of memory. The API needs about one core for 300 rps of mostly cached reads, the gateway under one core for 1,500 sockets, and Postgres sees a trickle because quotes come from Valkey. That is why the whole stack can run on a laptop, and why a two-hour load test on one rented VM is enough to show the targets hold. In production the API and gateway scale by adding copies; both are stateless apart from their sockets.

Full-market tick rates need a licensed feed. A free broker API streams a few thousand instruments, so the ingest and storage rows are exercised with the simulator.

§6Market data

Free to use, not free to show

For your own use, Indian market data can cost nothing. Exchanges publish end-of-day files, and several brokers give account holders a live WebSocket feed at no charge. Showing that data to the public is different: broker terms forbid it, and NSE licenses even delayed and end-of-day display. So in this project live data stays behind your own login, and the public demo runs on a simulator.

6.2The market simulator

A third ingestor adapter, SimulatorProvider, generates a whole market and publishes it through the same pipeline as real ticks. It is the only feed the public demo uses, it keeps the demo moving at night and at weekends, and it is how a load test reaches full-market tick rates that no free API allows.

  • Universe: the real instrument master (symbols, lot sizes, expiries) from NSE's files, so pages look right, with simulated prices.
  • Prices: a random walk anchored at each instrument's last real close, with volatility that clusters, intraday volume curves and occasional gaps.
  • Derivatives: futures from cost of carry, option premiums from the same Black-76 code the chain uses with a volatility smile, and open interest that drifts and builds near the money.
  • Controls: tick rate, instrument count and "event" injection (a 5% spike) from a small admin endpoint, so alert and load tests are repeatable.
  • Honesty on screen: every simulated quote carries a SIMULATED badge.

6.3If it ever became a product

Then the data licence would be the biggest cost of the whole business. On NSE's tariff from 1 April 2026, showing real-time prices on an open website costs ₹29.2 lakh a year per segment; 15-minute delayed costs ₹1.2 lakh per segment per medium (website and app are billed separately); end-of-day costs ₹1 lakh per segment. BSE charges ₹16 lakh for real-time on a website and ₹75,000 for delayed. Data would come from one of NSE's 26 authorised vendors, such as TrueData or Global Financial Datafeeds. Knowing these numbers, and why the design switches feeds by plan, is useful in an interview.

What this means for the design

  • Every feed goes through one MarketDataProvider interface: connect, subscribe, a tick callback, history and the instrument master. Three adapters implement it. BrokerProvider covers SmartAPI or Fyers with your own keys. ReplayProvider plays sessions you recorded for yourself. SimulatorProvider is the public one.
  • The gateway picks channels by plan and by source. Live broker channels are only ever served to your own account, and every quote on screen carries a LIVE, DELAYED, EOD or SIMULATED badge.
  • Entitlements are enforced on the server, in both the API and the gateway, the same way they would be for a licensed product.

§7Low-level design

7.1The live tick path

  • Bars close on exchange timestamps, not the server clock. Closed 1-minute bars are written with COPY every 5 seconds. After a restart, the ingestor backfills the gap from the source's history API and refreshes the rollups for that window.
  • One active ingestor. Two tasks race for a lease in Valkey (SET NX with a 10 s TTL, renewed every 3 s). The standby takes over within about 10 seconds.
  • Validation drops ticks outside the day's price band, zero prices and out-of-order sequence numbers, and counts every drop as a metric. Ticks outside a scheduled session (exchange mock sessions, test packets) are ignored.
  • New subscribers get the current snapshot immediately from the gateway's memory or the Valkey hash, then deltas.

7.2Stream protocol

// client → gateway: small JSON text frames
{"op":"auth",  "token":"<5-minute JWT from POST /v1/stream/token>"}
{"op":"sub",   "ids":[738561, 256265], "mode":"quote"}   // mode: ltp | quote | depth
{"op":"unsub", "ids":[256265]}

// gateway → client: binary Protobuf, one frame per flush with only what changed
message QuoteBatch { int64 ts_ms = 1; repeated Quote q = 2; }
message Quote {
  uint32 id = 1;                         // instrument id
  sint64 ltp = 2;  sint64 chg = 3;       // price × 10⁴, exact for 0.0025 currency ticks
  uint64 vol = 4;  uint64 oi = 5;
  sint64 o = 6; sint64 h = 7; sint64 l = 8; sint64 pc = 9;   // quote mode
  repeated Level bid = 10; repeated Level ask = 11;          // depth mode
  int64 exch_ts_ms = 12;
}
  • Authenticate within 5 seconds or the socket closes. The JWT carries the plan, so the gateway never calls the database.
  • Free plans: 50 instruments, 1-second frames, delayed channels. Paid plans: 500 or more instruments and 250 ms frames.
  • A slow client with more than 1 MB buffered skips frames, which loses nothing because each frame carries the latest state. After 30 seconds it is closed with code 4008. Clients reconnect with jittered backoff and resubscribe.

7.3Alert engine

// per instrument, in memory; loaded from app.alert at start, updated on change events
above[id]: min-heap of (threshold, alertId, version)     // fires when ltp ≥ top
below[id]: max-heap of (threshold, alertId, version)     // fires when ltp ≤ top

on quote(id, ltp):
  while above[id].top ≤ ltp: fire(above[id].pop())
  while below[id].top ≥ ltp: fire(below[id].pop())

fire(a):   // exactly-once even if two engines see the same tick
  UPDATE app.alert SET status = 'TRIGGERED', trigger_count = trigger_count + 1, last_triggered_at = now()
   WHERE id = $1 AND version = $2 AND status = 'ACTIVE' RETURNING user_id
  if a row came back: deliveries.add('deliver', { alertId, version, observed: ltp })   // BullMQ on Valkey
  • "Up 5% today" becomes a plain price level at 09:00 from the previous close, so percentage alerts reuse the same heaps. Volume and OI alerts use heaps on the day's cumulative figures.
  • Indicator alerts (RSI, moving-average crosses) are evaluated when a bar closes, never per tick. Screen-match and event alerts run after the EOD pipeline.
  • Heaps are O(log n) per fire. 1,000 users with 50 alerts each is 50k entries, which fits in a few megabytes.

7.4Option chain and Greeks

  • Model. Black-76 on the forward: the same-expiry future, or a synthetic forward from put-call parity at the ATM strike. The rate is the 91-day T-bill yield. Time runs to the 15:30 IST expiry in calendar-year fractions. Indian index options and stock options are European-style.
  • First paint comes from the API, with server-computed IV and Greeks cached for 1 second per underlying and expiry. After that the browser subscribes to every contract in view and recomputes IV and Greeks in a Web Worker as prices move. A 200-strike chain takes a few milliseconds.
  • One set of numbers. The TypeScript and Python implementations share a file of golden test vectors, so the chain, the screener and the historical IV charts agree to four decimal places.
  • History. Every minute a job writes one row per underlying and expiry to md.fo_chain_summary_1m: PCR, max pain, ATM IV, 25-delta skew and total OI.
  • 2025–26 rules in the data. NSE weekly expiries are Nifty 50 only, on Tuesdays; BSE's are Sensex only, on Thursdays. Position limits and the ban list use futures-equivalent (delta-adjusted) OI, stored as fut_eq_oi. Expiries, lot sizes (Nifty is now 65) and tick sizes all come from the instrument master and are never hard-coded.

7.5Screener

-- what the user types
mcap_cr > 5000 AND roce_pct > 20 AND (pe_ttm < 25 OR peg < 1) AND in_index('NIFTY 500')

-- parsed to an AST, every identifier checked against scr.metric, then compiled with bound parameters
SELECT instrument_id, mcap_cr, roce_pct, pe_ttm, peg
FROM   scr.equity_snapshot
WHERE  mcap_cr > $1 AND roce_pct > $2 AND (pe_ttm < $3 OR peg < $4) AND index_ids @> $5
ORDER BY mcap_cr DESC LIMIT 50;
  • Users never send SQL. At most 30 conditions and a depth of 5; a 2-second statement timeout; runs on the read replica.
  • The snapshot has one typed row per listed equity, about 5,000 rows. Fundamentals refresh after EOD; price-derived columns (market cap, P/E, returns) refresh every minute in session.
  • Results are cached for 60 seconds by a hash of the AST, so a popular screen runs once a minute however many people open it.

7.9Correctness rules

  • Daily bars come from bhavcopies. 1-minute-derived OHLC is reconciled against them each night, and mismatches go to ops.data_quality_issue. F&O stocks' official closes now come from the closing auction.
  • History is never rewritten for corporate actions. Price factors are applied at read time in three modes: none, capital (splits, bonuses, rights) and total return.
  • Calendars, sessions, expiries, lot sizes and tick sizes are data. All of them changed in 2025–26 (Tuesday expiries, a closing auction, price-band ticks, new lot sizes), and they will change again.
  • Time. Timestamps are stored in UTC and shown in IST. Business dates are IST dates. The fiscal year runs April to March, so Q1 FY27 is April–June 2026.
  • Idempotent jobs. Upserts on natural keys, and a (job, business date) row in ops.job_run that allows one success per day.

  • One engine, two feeds. The Python engine walks bars in time order and keeps positions, cash and open orders. Backtest workers feed it history from TimescaleDB; the paper-trading runner feeds it each 1-minute bar as the ingestor closes it. Indicators are precomputed column-wise with polars, so the bar loop stays cheap.
  • No look-ahead. Signals use a bar's close and fill at the next bar's open. When one bar touches both the stop and the target, the engine assumes the stop was hit first.
  • Point in time. Universe membership, lot sizes, expiries, price bands and ban days come from the validity-ranged tables in §8, and charges come from ref.charge_rate for each trade date. The same weekly-options rules expire on Thursdays in 2024 and on Tuesdays in 2026, because each contract carries its real expiry date.
  • Reproducible. Each run records its data version and engine version. The cache key hashes those with the strategy version, parameters and range, so an identical request returns the stored result at once.
// API (Node): record the run, then queue its id; the run id is also the job id, so a retry cannot enqueue twice

  • A queue for dispatch, a table for the record. lab.backtest_run keeps inputs, status, timings and results; BullMQ handles priorities, retries and stalled-job recovery, and its progress events reach the user's socket through the gateway. If Valkey ever loses a job, a reconciler re-enqueues runs still marked QUEUED in Postgres. This is ADR 0001 in the repo.
  • Data honesty. Daily equity and F&O history comes free from archived bhavcopies, including every option strike's daily close. Intraday history, above all for options, only exists from the day you start recording, so the ingestor records from phase 0. Simulated prices are refused as backtest input because they would only test the simulator.
  • Plans. The trading-plan checker runs on every paper fill and at the close, and records breaches of risk per trade, daily loss or position count in the journal. The investment planner replays the user's SIP over rolling historical windows of the chosen index and reports the spread of outcomes, not a single projected number.

Why it is shaped this way

  • Issuer → security → instrument separates who issued something, what was issued and where it trades. A share listed on NSE and BSE is one security with two instruments. A Nifty option is an instrument whose underlying is the Nifty 50 index instrument.
  • Identifiers with validity ranges. Tokens, symbols and ISINs sit in tables guarded by exclusion constraints, so a code can never point at two instruments on the same day, and history survives renames and face-value splits. Token types are namespaced per segment because NSE's cash and F&O token spaces overlap.
  • Financials stored long. Statement → line item → value, against one dictionary that covers the separate bank, NBFC and insurer layouts. A restatement adds a version, and a partial unique index allows exactly one latest version per period.
  • The screener reads one wide, typed row per stock, so filters stay fast and simple at about 5,000 rows.
  • Market-data tables have no foreign keys; user tables do. Market data can move to its own cluster later without touching the app.
  • Point in time by construction. Validity ranges on identifiers, index membership and charges, per-contract lot sizes and expiries, and daily status rows let a backtest see the market as it was, delisted stocks included.
  • Checks use CASE where a NULL could slip through. A bare OR in a CHECK passes when one side is NULL. The smoke test caught two such gaps while this schema was written, including a future that could carry a strike.

Both files are verified. The core schema runs on PostgreSQL 17, and the Timescale layer on TimescaleDB 2.30.1, where it creates 7 hypertables, 2 continuous aggregates and 9 background policies without a warning. The 35 smoke checks pass on both, and CI repeats them on every push.
